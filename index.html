1. What Live Earth does

Live Earth offers an interactive 3D globe, maps, satellite imagery, weather, news, road and traffic information, links to cameras, and searches for nearby places and emergency services. Many features obtain information from independent online providers. Live Earth Premium offers a monthly or yearly subscription through Google Play.

2. Approximate location from your internet connection

When the main Live Earth globe opens, the app requests an approximate city or region from ipwho.is using its internet connection. This happens without requesting Android GPS permission. Your public IP address and ordinary request information can be visible to ipwho.is. The resulting approximate location may be inaccurate and is not necessarily your actual location.

This information helps position the starting globe and support location-relevant content. The audited V105 app does not contain a developer-operated server that deliberately records the public IP address or stores location histories.

3. GPS is optional

GPS is off by default. If you select a location feature, Android may ask you for foreground approximate or precise location permission. With permission, the app can request a location fix to centre the globe/map, identify nearby services, or retrieve information for that area. Some such requests send coordinates or location-related search terms to outside providers listed below.

Live Earth does not request background location permission in the reviewed V105 build. It is not intended to track you after closing the app. You can decline location permission and use other available features. You can also change or revoke location permission in your phone's Settings. Turning GPS off does not prevent the approximate IP-based lookup described above when the main globe opens.

4. Third-party information providers and requests

When you use relevant features, internet requests can reveal a device's public IP address, viewed map region, search terms or geographical coordinates to the receiving provider. Examples present in the reviewed V105 code include:

  • ipwho.is: approximate starting location based on your internet connection.
  • Open-Meteo: forecast and geocoding requests for weather or place selection.
  • OpenStreetMap-based services, public Overpass servers and Photon: place and emergency-service searches, map/location results and suggestions.
  • Esri ArcGIS and EOX: satellite maps, globe imagery and the region of Earth viewed.
  • NASA GIBS, Copernicus data, Element 84 Earth Search and associated imagery delivery: global and recent satellite imagery requests for selected map areas.
  • Queensland traffic and fuel data services: relevant traffic, road or fuel information.
  • News services, including publisher feeds and Google News RSS: regional news queries and requested articles.
  • Google Maps and camera/news provider websites: when you open external results, those destinations can receive the place, link or coordinates contained in the URL.

Looking at an area on the globe does not necessarily mean you are physically there. Independent providers may process internet requests, use cookies on their own sites, and retain logs under their own privacy policies. Live Earth cannot control those independent providers' retention periods.

5. Google Play subscriptions and billing

Live Earth Premium offers recurring monthly and yearly subscriptions. Purchases and payment-method information are handled by Google Play, according to Google's policies. The app connects to the Google Play Billing service to display plans and prices, start purchases, and check or restore subscription status. The reviewed V105 internal-testing build can receive purchase status and a purchase token via that service but does not include a separate developer-operated billing-verification server.

Live Earth does not ask for or store your full payment card number in its own app code. You can manage or cancel a Google Play subscription in your Google Play account. An active subscription is needed for access to premium features; internal debug previews are for testing and are not a consumer payment arrangement.

6. Information held on your device

Live Earth uses temporary application/session state for navigation, map and location features. Some imagery, map information and other responses may be cached to improve performance. Android WebView can keep ordinary browser-style caches or local storage (for example, certain interface preferences) on your phone.

The reviewed V105 build has Android app backup disabled and does not have a separate Live Earth account registration, developer-run advertising profile database, or developer-operated GPS tracking server. Other apps and websites opened from Live Earth may keep their own information under their own policies.

7. Security and how long data is kept

Live Earth uses HTTPS for the app's reviewed main online service requests and relies on Android/Google Play security features for permission handling and payments. We aim to request information only as needed for app features. App caches and WebView storage may persist on the device until cleared; in-memory information is typically lost when the app process ends. Outside providers have their own logging and retention rules.

To clear the data held on your phone by Live Earth, use Android Settings → Apps → Live Earth → Storage → Clear data, or uninstall the app. You can revoke location permissions in Android Settings. This does not remove information independent providers may have retained. Because the reviewed app has no Live Earth account system, there is no separate Live Earth account to delete. Contact us at the address above regarding information controlled by the developer.

8. External websites and third-party content

Some headlines, map/search results and camera entries lead to a publisher's website or another app. Once you leave Live Earth, that destination may use its own cookies, analytics and privacy policies. We do not control those websites.

9. Advertising and analytics

The reviewed V105 app code does not include an app-operated behavioural advertising network, user account sign-up system or a dedicated third-party analytics SDK. This does not mean independent map, news, camera, Google Play or webpage providers never log requests. If Live Earth later introduces advertising, accounts, analytics or server-side subscriptions, this policy will be updated before those changes are released.

10. Your rights, choices and questions

You may decline or revoke optional location permission, avoid opening external links, cancel subscriptions through Google Play, and clear local app data. Email liveearth.support@gmail.com with privacy concerns or requests relating to information controlled by the Live Earth developer. Requests about information retained by another service may need to go directly to that provider.

11. Updates to this policy

We may update this Privacy Policy as Live Earth changes, including changes to its subscriptions, data providers and security architecture. Material changes will be reflected on this page, with a revised effective date.

This policy describes the reviewed V105 internal-test implementation as at 14 October 2026. Public distribution depends on completion of the remaining service-licensing, purchase-security and data-handling checks.
